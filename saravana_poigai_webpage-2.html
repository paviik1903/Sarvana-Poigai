What is Saravana Poigai?

Saravana Poigai is a sacred pond connected with Lord Murugan, also known as Skanda, Kartikeya or Subramanya. In Hindu tradition, the name is closely linked with the story of Murugan’s divine birth.

The word poigai in Tamil means a pond or water pool. Sacred ponds are important in many Indian temple traditions: they are places for prayer, reflection and ritual observances. The name Saravana Poigai is especially remembered in stories about how Lord Murugan came into the world.

The traditional legend

According to a well-known Hindu legend, Lord Shiva’s divine energy appeared as six sparks of fire. The sparks were carried by Agni, the fire deity, and associated with the waters of the Ganga. They were brought to a sacred reed-filled pond called Saravana Poigai.

1

Six divine sparks

Six sparks emerged from Shiva’s divine power, symbolising extraordinary energy.

2

The sacred pond

The sparks were brought to Saravana Poigai, where they became six infant boys.

3

The Krittikas

The six infants were cared for by the Krittika maidens, who are associated with the Pleiades star cluster.

4

Shanmukha

Goddess Parvati embraced the six children, uniting them as one deity with six faces, known as Shanmukha or Arumugam.

Remember: This is a sacred religious legend preserved through Hindu tradition and devotional literature. It is not a scientifically verified account of a historical event.

Religious and cultural significance

  • Connection with Lord Murugan: Saravana Poigai is remembered as a place connected to his divine birth.
  • Unity: The six infants becoming one deity is often understood as a symbol of unity.
  • Devotion: The story is important to many devotees, especially in Tamil-speaking communities where Murugan worship has a long tradition.
  • Temple heritage: Sacred ponds and temple tanks form part of the religious and cultural landscape of many South Indian temples.

There are places called Saravana Poigai associated with Murugan temples, including locations at Thirupparankundram and Palani in Tamil Nadu. It is useful to distinguish the mythological Saravana Poigai in the birth story from particular temple ponds visited by devotees today.
